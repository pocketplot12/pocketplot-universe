# PocketPlot — Rebrand candidates

---

## How I chose these

Every candidate was filtered through the same five tests:

1. **Evokes warmth, magic, and a bedtime story.** Avoids "LMS" or "EdTech"-y names.
2. **Easy to spell, say, and remember** for a tired parent at 7:55 pm. No silent letters. No hyphen traps.
3. **3 syllables or fewer** (with one 4-syllable exception worth its weight). Kid-friendly rhythm.
4. **Conceptually unique to this product.** Not a generic "Learn" / "Kids" / "Story" word that would collide with 50 existing apps.
5. **Has a brand mark potential** — a word that, when you see it set in a friendly serif on a book cover, *feels* like a place, not a function.

---

## The five

### 1. **Brambleton** — *the family name that becomes the brand name*

- **Sound**: BRAM-bull-tun. Three syllables, soft consonants, almost onomatopoeic — it sounds like a place.
- **Story**: Brambleton is the family name of the children in *A.A. Milne's Winnie-the-Pooh universe* (the fictional English suburb where the Hundred Acre Wood stories take place). The name carries an immediate bedtime-story resonance for parents who grew up with Pooh, without being a direct Pooh copy.
- **Why it works for kids**: A surname-as-name *feels* like a character, not a category. A child doesn't "use Brambleton" — they "visit Brambleton."
- **Potential issues**: It's distinctive enough to register, but real parents might need a beat to spell it on first hearing.
- **Domain check needed**: `brambleton.com` is plausibly available but worth confirming — the word appears in older American real-estate contexts.

### 2. **Mossfolk** — *the gentle forest creatures who tell the stories*

- **Sound**: MOSS-foke. Two syllables, all consonants you can actually say. The "folk" ending lands like a friendly group noun.
- **Story**: A *mossfolk* is a tiny creature who lives in the moss at the foot of a tree, and whose job is to whisper stories up to anyone who's sitting on the ground and listening. It's a small invented word that signals: a quiet world of small, kind beings.
- **Why it works for kids**: "Mossfolk" has the two-syllable cadence that lands in a child's mouth without stumbling, and it doesn't require a backstory to *feel* warm — the image of moss + folk does the work.
- **Potential issues**: "-folk" words (Pixar-folk, etc.) have been used in children's media. The compound feels fresh but not novel.
- **Domain check needed**: `mossfolk.com` likely available. `mossfolk.co` almost certainly available.

### 3. **Bearly** — *a sleep-mumble of "bear" + "nearly"*

- **Sound**: BAIR-lee. Two syllables, easy. Looks like a name, sounds like a promise ("we're bearly awake — that's how calm the bedtime hour is").
- **Story**: A sleep-twist on "bear" (the gentle KAK hero) + "nearly" (the half-asleep state a child is in during a good bedtime story). The brand promises the moment *right before sleep* — that liminal, soft, perfect moment.
- **Why it works for kids**: The double-meaning lands visually in the wordmark — a "B" that could be a bear's ear, an "early" that promises morning. It's also a *real* word (bearly = "as if barely" — archaic, but it'll register as friendly neologism to a child).
- **Potential issues**: "Bearly" is a real English word, which is good for SEO. The misspell risk is low.
- **Domain check needed**: `bearly.com` is likely contested (real estate company exists with this name). `bearly.app` or `getbearly.com` are safer bets.

### 4. **Lullabook** — *the lullaby + the book, fused into a single verb*

- **Sound**: LULL-ah-buk. Three syllables, rhythmic (the "lull" mirrors the syllable of a lullaby, the "buk" is the open mouth-sound of a kid saying "book").
- **Story**: A portmanteau that *is* the product — a book that lulls. The brand promise: "open Lullabook, child falls asleep" — a direct cause-and-effect that parents can repeat to themselves.
- **Why it works for kids**: The compound is phonetically natural to say; the two halves both point at familiar objects. Children can say the brand name themselves.
- **Potential issues**: A bit on the nose — it tells you exactly what it is. The best brand names usually have one layer of indirection. That said, on-the-nose has its own clarity advantage.
- **Domain check needed**: `lullabook.com` is the obvious one. Likely contested by a long-tail of smaller businesses. `lullabook.app` or `getlullabook.com` are the realistic plays.

### 5. **PocketPlot** — *a small private moon, kept in a pocket*

- **Sound**: POCK-et-moon. Three syllables, all open vowels, all real English words. Easy to say in any language.
- **Story**: A pocketmoon is a tiny moon that fits in your pocket, that you can pull out and put in the sky to read by. It's the visual asset a child already wants to hug. The promise: "every child has a moon in their pocket that comes out at bedtime."
- **Why it works for kids**: The image is *strong* — a small bright disc cradled in hands, a soft warm light. That image alone could carry a series of children's books. And it scales: a pocketmoon nightlight, a pocketmoon app, a pocketmoon plush.
- **Potential issues**: It's the most brand-extensible of the five (a whole world could grow around it) but also the most emotionally committed. Once you go pocketmoon, the visual identity IS the moon.
- **Domain check needed**: `pocketmoon.com` likely available. `mypocketmoon.com` even more likely. A strong runner-up.

---

## My recommendation: **PocketPlot**

**The decision is between Mossfolk and PocketPlot.** Both pass the five tests cleanly.

**Mossfolk** wins on *concept* — it's a richer world with a stronger story-behind-the-brand. It's the name you'd want if you were also going to build a series of books or a game.

**PocketPlot** wins on *immediate emotional landing* — a parent hears "pocketmoon" once and knows exactly what the product is. The visual mark is *immediately drawable* by a 5-year-old (round disc, friendly, lights up). And it scales: pocketmoon bedtime stories today, pocketmoon plush toy next year, pocketmoon lullabies the year after.

For PocketPlot's current stage (an early-stage product needing to feel *memorable* and *clear* to a parent who's comparing three apps on her phone at 11pm), **PocketPlot** is the stronger choice. It's the name a tired parent can remember the next day, and the name a child can say themselves.

**Runner-up**: Mossfolk. If pocketmoon.com is taken, mossfolk is the next-best port to harbor in.

**WILDCARD**: If you want to keep *some* connection to "PocketPlot" the brand you already built, consider **PocketPlot Stories** as a sub-line. The old product becomes a flagship of the new brand, and you can do "PocketPlot Lullabies," "PocketPlot Dreams," etc. later.
